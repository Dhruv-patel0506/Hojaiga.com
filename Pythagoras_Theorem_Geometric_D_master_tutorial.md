# Hojaiga Master Tutorial: Pythagoras Theorem Geometric Derivation

1. **VISUAL DIAGRAM TRACING:**
   - The diagram shows a right triangle with legs labeled as \( a \) and \( b \), and the hypotenuse labeled as \( c \).
   - The formula \( a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \) is written above the triangle.

2. **CORE PRINCIPLE:**
   - The Pythagorean Theorem states that in a right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides.
   - This theorem is fundamental in geometry and has numerous real-world applications, such as in construction, surveying, and navigation.

3. **STEP-BY-STEP PROOF / DERIVATION:**
   - Consider a right triangle with legs \( a \) and \( b \), and hypotenuse \( c \).
   - Drop a perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse, creating two smaller right triangles.
   - By the properties of similar triangles, the ratio of the sides of the smaller triangles is the same as the ratio of the sides of the original triangle.
   - Therefore, \( \frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{c} \), which implies \( a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \).

4. **COGNITIVE HURDLE DIAGNOSIS:**
   - A common confusion point is understanding the geometric relationship between the sides of the triangle and the derived equation.
   - Another potential hurdle is remembering the specific formula and its application in different geometric contexts.

5. **WORKED PRACTICE EXAMPLE:**
   - **Problem:** In a right triangle, if \( a = 3 \) and \( b = 4 \), find \( c \).
   - **Solution:**
     1. Apply the Pythagorean Theorem: \( a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \).
     2. Substitute the given values: \( 3^2 + 4^2 = c^2 \).
     3. Simplify: \( 9 + 16 = c^2 \).
     4. Combine like terms: \( 25 = c^2 \).
     5. Take the square root of both sides: \( c = \sqrt{25} \).
     6. Simplify: \( c = 5 \).
   - **Takeaway Rule:** Always use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse when the lengths of the other two sides are known.
