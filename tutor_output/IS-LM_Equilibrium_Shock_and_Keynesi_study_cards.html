IS-LM Equilibrium Shock and Keynesian Liquidity Trap Dynamics

### TIER 1: MULTIMODAL PERCEPTION & DIAGRAM DECONSTRUCTION

**IS-LM Equilibrium Shock and Keynesian Liquidity Trap Dynamics Diagram:**

1. **IS Curve (Investment-Savings Curve):**
   - Represents the relationship between interest rates and output (GDP).
   - Slope: Negative (as interest rates rise, investment decreases).
   - Y-intercept: GDP at full employment.

2. **LM Curve (Liquidity Preference-Interest Rate Curve):**
   - Represents the relationship between interest rates and output.
   - Slope: Positive (as interest rates rise, money demand increases).
   - Y-intercept: GDP at full employment.

3. **Keynesian Liquidity Trap:**
   - Occurs when the LM curve is vertical at low interest rates.
   - This implies that the money market is perfectly liquid at low interest rates.

4. **IS-LM Equilibrium:**
   - Intersection of IS and LM curves determines the equilibrium interest rate and output.
   - Keynesian Liquidity Trap: When the LM curve is vertical, the IS curve shifts to the right, leading to a new equilibrium with higher output but no change in interest rates.

### TIER 2: FIRST-PRINCIPLES INTUITION & MENTAL MODELS

**Real-World Analogies:**

1. **IS-LM Equilibrium:**
   - Imagine a seesaw where the left side represents investment and the right side represents savings. The seesaw balances when the number of people on each side is equal.
   - The IS curve shows how changes in interest rates affect investment, while the LM curve shows how changes in interest rates affect money demand.

2. **Keynesian Liquidity Trap:**
   - Think of a bank with a fixed amount of cash reserves. When the central bank lowers interest rates, the bank can't lend out more money because it has no more reserves.
   - This creates a liquidity trap where the economy can't expand further even with lower interest rates.
